1 / Context

A central problem in computational immunology

  1. 1Proteasome shreds cellular proteins into short peptide fragments (8–11 residues).
    ML analogy: a tokeniser that segments a protein into fixed-length inputs.
  2. 2MHC-I groove on the cell surface either accepts or rejects each peptide based on shape fit.
    ML analogy: a fixed-shape binary classifier — the allele is the context key, the peptide is the candidate.
  3. 3CD8+ T-cells patrol all cells, detecting foreign peptides and triggering destruction.
    ML analogy: distributed anomaly detection across every cell in the body.

The computational task

Given an HLA allele (context key), rank 10²–104 candidate peptides by binding likelihood. This is a ranking problem with ~13,000 known allele variants.

Why computational prediction?

Wet-lab validation of peptide–MHC binding is slow and expensive. A reliable in-silico ranker filters 104 candidates down to 101–102 worth testing. The false-positive rate of the upstream ranker is the bottleneck.

4 / The evaluation problem

Why reported gains in this field are hard to trust

Problem 1: Train–test leakage

NetMHCpan’s training corpus (~160K peptide–HLA pairs) overlaps with most public benchmarks. Without removal, test-set gains reflect memorisation, not generalisation.

Kapoor & Narayanan 2023 · Bernett et al. 2024

Problem 2: Unadjusted multiple testing

Benchmarks score across dozens of HLA alleles, each treated as independent. With 100 alleles at α=0.05, ~5 chance “wins” appear by noise alone.

FWER correction (Holm 1979) widely ignored in this literature.

Contamination ledger: TransPHLA shares 26.9% with NMP-4.1 corpus; IEDB auto_bench 15–22%.

5 / Our evaluation contract

The three-condition superiority rule

A signed Δ (DeepNeo − baseline) is reported as a real win only if all three conditions hold:

  1. 1Paired DeLong p < 0.05 on the same rows.
    Tests whether pooled AUROC difference is non-zero.
  2. 2Per-allele Wilcoxon signed-rank matches the pooled Δ sign, with Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 across the family of four TransPHLA comparisons.
    Guards against cherry-picking a single allele group.
  3. 3Allele-clustered bootstrap 95% CI on Δ excludes zero (B = 1,000; n = 118,143 rows).
    Accounts for allele-level non-independence in the sample.

Preceded by exact-pair decontamination

Every evaluation row also in NetMHCpan-4.1’s training-pair union is removed before scoring. On TransPHLA: 43,509 of 161,652 rows removed (26.9%) → 118,143 clean rows.

CS analogy: three-gate CI pipeline

Gate 1 (DeLong) catches point-estimate noise. Gate 2 (Holm) catches allele-level multiplicity. Gate 3 (bootstrap CI) catches sample-dependence. A “win” passes all three gates or gets rejected.

Outcome verdicts

Gates passedVerdict
All 3 pass, Δ > 0WIN
All 3 pass, Δ < 0LOSS
Any gate failsTIED

Why this matters

Without multiplicity control, ~5 of 100 alleles show “wins” by chance at α=0.05. Our rule eliminates cherry-picking: every claim is Holm-adjusted across the full family.

8 / Honest reporting

Where NetMHCpan still wins

BA-head vs BA-head

Δ AUROC = −0.0137 — NMP higher

Holm-adjusted p = 1.7 × 10−3 · Bootstrap CI excludes zero

All three conditions met → Holm-robust NMP win

Different protocols → different verdicts

Without Holm adjustment, the fusion’s pooled edge (+0.0073) can be reported as a win. Under the three-condition rule it is not Holm-robust (Holm p = 0.151).

Why report this?

Most papers in this field report only wins. We apply the same rule uniformly: NMP’s BA-head advantage is real and survives all three gates. The fusion’s EL-driven recovery is what closes the deployed gap.

9 / Case study · evaluation-leakage failure

A documented transductive-overfitting failure

We fit a second-stage pocket rescorer by 5-fold CV on the evaluation benchmark itself.

0.862
in-sample AUROC
(transductive)
→
0.442
held-out alleles
(inductive) · below chance

What happened

Fitting an inference-time head on the evaluation set itself is leakage that ordinary train/test hygiene does not catch. The gain is in-sample; the collapse is consistent with evaluation-set fitting, not transferable discrimination.

Component withdrawn and reported as a documented negative.

10 / Takeaway

What this paper actually claims

Model

A compact 35M-parameter PLM for peptide–MHC class I ranking, trained on the official NetMHCpan-4.1 folds.

ESM2 trunk + cross-attention · 5-fold ensemble · SupCon refinement

Protocol

A leakage-aware, multiplicity-controlled evaluation contract, applied verbatim in Results and linked to the methodology literature.

DeLong + Holm-Wilcoxon + Bootstrap CI · pair-level decontamination

Negatives

Two evaluation-failure case studies on our own data: a successor-corpus holdout overlap and a transductive-overfitting rescorer collapse.

7 withdrawn components · documented as negatives

Scope restrictions

Class I MHC · canonical 8–11-mer peptides · no clinical, Class II, PTM, or TCR-specificity claim.
